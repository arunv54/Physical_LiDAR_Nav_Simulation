01 / The question

What can a moving
3D LiDAR actually see?

Controlled evaluations connect sensor position, sampling density, target characteristics, and atmospheric conditions to measurements in one fixed obstacle scene.

The project models a spinning, pulsed LiDAR carried by a ground robot. A point appears only when a beam samples a surface, reaches it without obstruction, and returns enough optical power. This report follows that chain from geometry to measured maps.

04 / Sensor state and configuration

PDF §§3.1 & 4.2 · Figure 3

Change the available information.

Sensor state is position and orientation; sampling density is a configuration parameter, and diffuse reflectance is a target property. We distinguish these inputs and evaluate how each enters the same measurement model.

Sensor state

Height and changing viewpoint

An independent ground-plane sweep checks range against r = −h / sin β. The moving replay changes sensor position and target range. All four cases use exactly the same recorded position and heading at each time.

Sensor configuration

Dense and sparse sampling

360 versus 90 azimuth samples, eight channels, the same full-circle FOV, 0.6 m mount, zero mounting angles, receiver noise and atmosphere. Emitted beams change from 2,880 to 720 per scan.

Target feature

Bright and weak diffuse returns

Only the designated target’s diffuse coefficient changes: 0.85 versus 0.00005. This weak-return setting is an illustrative stress case, rather than a calibrated material. Geometry and other materials remain fixed.

What this demonstrates

At mean SNR 10, the model predicts a 50% detection probability. The three saved estimates are 51.0%, 49.2%, and 49.8%. The experiment checks the assumed noise law where detection is genuinely uncertain.

Geometry, target diffuse weight, receiver noise, and threshold remain fixed within each viewpoint; illustrative extinction changes mean SNR. Conditional range errors use detected returns only. This establishes internal model consistency, not physical LiDAR accuracy.

The same task under three atmospheric settings

What this demonstrates

All nine robots reach the goal, while mean observed cells fall from 73.7% in clear air to 70.3% in rain and 47.1% under fog extinction. Similar trajectories and completion times can coexist with substantially different sensing quality.

Three paired seeds support a controlled comparison in this scene; they do not establish broad navigation reliability. The rain and fog settings model attenuation only, without atmospheric backscatter, droplet false returns, or real-weather calibration. No cross-layout or obstacle-change evaluation is included.

What this demonstrates

In the same fixed scene, mean endpoint-observed cells are 73.7% in clear air, 70.3% in rain, and 47.1% under fog extinction. All three conditions achieve 3/3 goals, while their measured maps contain different amounts of observed surface.

Only measured endpoints are drawn; no true scene surfaces fill gaps. Display is sampled to 16,000 original voxels per run, while map counts use all saved voxels and cells. Robot poses are known exactly: this is measured-surface accumulation, not pose estimation or SLAM. Observed endpoint area does not certify free space, traversability, or map accuracy.

09 / Conclusions & limits

PDF §5 & References

A chain of evidence, with clear limits.

What the evidence supports

  • Pose and FOV change beam intersections.
  • Opaque surfaces constrain LOS before power and detection.
  • Range, target response, and atmosphere change optical power.
  • Fixed-angle sampling and target-reflectance controls separate geometric sampling from threshold detection.
  • Independent threshold trials match the assumed probabilistic model.
  • Atmospheric attenuation reduces measured points and observed map coverage even when the same navigation task succeeds.

What remains outside the evidence

  • Physical LiDAR calibration and measured-device accuracy.
  • Calibrated weather, atmospheric backscatter, and complex materials.
  • Pose error, moving targets, navigation scan motion, latency, and acceleration.
  • Reliability across randomly sampled environments or other controllers.

Separate analytical checks cover 65 geometry cases and 72 optical-power cases, with maximum residuals 3.83 × 10⁻¹³ m and 4.39 × 10⁻¹⁶ relative power. Agreement is a numerical consistency check of the stated assumptions.

Data sources and presentation method

This page presents the saved measurements and equations behind the report. Changing a control selects a recorded case, replays recorded measurements, or evaluates an explicitly labelled analytical relationship. It does not run the sensor, planner, or navigation experiments.
